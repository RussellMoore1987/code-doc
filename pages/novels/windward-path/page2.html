Chapter 1: The Road Ahead

The morning Sable Voss accepted the last commission of her life, the sky was the color of an old bruise — purple at the edges, yellow-grey at the center, as though the dawn itself was uncertain whether to arrive.

She stood in the dispatch yard of the Irongate Courier House with a leather satchel slung over one shoulder and a sealed letter tucked inside her coat, close against her ribs. The letter had no name on it. Only a wax seal she did not recognize: a crow with its wings folded, sitting on a crescent moon.

The courier master, a broad man named Aldric with a nose broken three times in three different directions, pressed two silver marks into her palm and closed her fingers around them without meeting her eyes.

"Greenhollow," he said. "Don't open it. Don't talk about it. Don't stop."

"I never stop," Sable told him.

"You stop for water."

"I stop for water."

He almost smiled. "Greenhollow in four days. Bonus if you make it in three. Penalty if you don't make it at all."

Sable looked at the coins in her hand. "What kind of penalty?"

Aldric turned back to his ledger. "The kind you don't negotiate."
