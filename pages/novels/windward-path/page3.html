She left the city by the south gate before the watch changed, moving fast enough that her breath made small clouds in the cold air. The road to Greenhollow ran through the Anford valley — three days of forest and clay, then one day of open highland if the weather held.

It didn't hold.

By midday the sky had gone the color of iron shavings and the wind had shifted to come straight off the peaks. Sable pulled her hood up and kept her head down and tried not to think about the letter pressing against her ribs like a second heartbeat.

She thought about it anyway.

In six years of carrying messages she had never once opened a seal. This had less to do with professional ethics than with the understanding that couriers who read their cargo had a way of disappearing. Still, there was something different about this one. A weight to it that had nothing to do with paper or wax. A stillness, the way deep water is still.

She was three miles south of the city when she heard the second set of footsteps behind her.

They were careful. Not careful enough.

Sable did not break her stride. She let her hand fall to the short knife at her belt and kept her eyes on the road and thought: Aldric, you absolute coward, what have you sent me into?
