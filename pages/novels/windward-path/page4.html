Chapter 2: Into the Storm

The man following her was good. He'd chosen soft soles and kept to the verge where the grass muffled sound. If Sable hadn't spent three years running night routes through Irongate's lower districts — where listening was the difference between arriving and not — she would have missed him entirely.

She turned off the main road at the old miller's track, a narrow path that wound down into a ravine thick with wet alder. If he followed, she'd hear him on the loose stones. If he didn't, he was waiting for someone else ahead.

He followed.

Sable stopped in the shadow of a fallen beech, back to bark, knife out, and waited. The footsteps slowed. He'd lost her pace and was trying to place her. She counted his breaths — three long ones, uncertain — and then she stepped out and put the knife against his throat before he could speak.

He was younger than she'd expected. Barely past twenty, with a courier's satchel of his own slung across his chest and the wide, still eyes of someone who has decided not to struggle.

"I'm not here to take it," he said.

"Then what are you here for?"

He swallowed carefully, mindful of the blade. "To warn you. The letter you're carrying — whoever receives it in Greenhollow will be dead before morning if you deliver it."

The wind moved through the alders. Somewhere above them, the first drops of rain began to fall.

"Then why," Sable said slowly, "does Aldric want it delivered?"

The boy looked at her with something that might have been relief, or might have been pity. "That," he said, "is exactly the right question."
