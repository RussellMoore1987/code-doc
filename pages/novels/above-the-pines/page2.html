Chapter One - Above the Pines

Runa had spent the better part of an hour trying not to walk too close to Kaelen.

This was proving considerably more difficult than it ought to have been.

The trail was narrow enough, there were plenty of reasonable excuses. Roots crossed the path in great wooden knuckles. Ferns crowded the edges. In places, ancient pines leaned inward until their branches knitted together overhead and turned the afternoon sunlight into long shafts of gold.

She could have blamed any of those things.

Instead, she maintained a respectable distance between them.

Mostly.

Kaelen walked beside her with the infuriating ease of someone who had never once in his life wondered what to do with his hands.

His white hair caught the sunlight whenever they passed beneath an opening in the canopy. Against his charcoal-dark skin it looked almost luminous. He walked quietly, boots finding their way over stone and root without conscious effort, his olive-green tunic shifting gently in the mountain breeze.

His boots found their places between stone and root as though he had already considered each step long before taking it.

He seemed perfectly content.

Calm.

At peace.

Runa found this deeply inconvenient.

We've been spending more time together.

That had to mean something.

Didn't it?

He likes me. Obviously he likes me.

The problem was determining precisely how he liked her.

More than a friend?

Or merely as a particularly entertaining friend who occasionally wandered through forests with him!?

With almost anyone else, Runa would have known.

Kaelen was different.

He listened too well.

He absorbed teasing comments, lingering looks, accidental touches that perhaps had not been entirely accidental—and somehow gave away almost nothing unless he wanted to.

Always that calm expression.

Always those amber-gold eyes that seemed to notice considerably more than he ever explained.

She stole another glance at him.

Kaelen was looking ahead.

Good.

Runa faced forward again and mustering a good amount of courage she said.

“You know,”

“I've really enjoyed spending more time with you.”

There. Easy. Completely ordinary. Nothing dangerous about those words. Runa clasped her hands behind her back and concentrated very seriously on a pine tree several hundred feet ahead. “It’s been really nice.” Her heart, now attempting to escape through her ribs. For half a heartbeat Kaelen said nothing. That half-heartbeat became, in Runa’s mind, an age in which kingdoms rose and fell. Then Kaelen turned toward her. Their eyes met. Something changed in his expression. It wasn't dramatic. Kaelen rarely did anything dramatically unless circumstances demanded it. But his attention settled completely upon her, and a slow warmth entered his eyes. Then he smiled. Not the easy smile he gave when she teased him. Something quieter. Deeper. For one brief moment, it felt as though all the calmness he normally kept wrapped around himself had opened just enough for her to see what was beneath it. “I agree,” he said. Runa forgot about the pine tree. His smile deepened slightly. “It’s been really nice spending more time with you.” A magnificent response. Poets would sing of it. Runa looked at the ground, a visible blush crossing her cheeks. A moment ago, or at least when they started the hike she had been perfectly capable of speaking. Now the forest floor had apparently become fascinating. There were pine needles. Several rocks. One especially ambitious beetle. She smiled despite herself. Kaelen had been the one to suggest the hike. He had said there was somewhere he wanted to show her, if she was up to it. That last part still amused her. They continued walking. For perhaps twenty paces, neither spoke. Runa could feel Kaelen beside her. Not looking at her. Probably. Although lately she had caught him doing that more often. Little glances. Nothing obvious. Nothing she could accuse him of. But they were different from before, newer, nicer, more somehow. Longer sometimes. Softer. As though he had started looking at her and then forgotten that he ought to look somewhere else. Runa glanced sideways. Kaelen was looking ahead. She looked forward again. Another ten paces. This was ridiculous Runa thought to herself. I am not timid. She had held her own in conversations and debates with scholars and officials twice her age. She had traveled farther than most people expected of her, faced difficult situations without losing her head, and earned the trust of people whose respect was not easily given. Yet one kind sentence from Kaelen had apparently reduced her to studying beetles. Fine. If words had betrayed her, she would use something simpler. She allowed the distance between them to narrow. One step. Then another. Kaelen seemed not to notice. Or pretended not to. Runa fought back a smile. Then, with all the dignity appropriate to a woman of her maturity and considerable accomplishments, she bumped him with her hip. Kaelen’s steps shifted ever so slightly. Runa immediately looked straight ahead. Nothing had happened. Nothing whatsoever. The trees were especially lovely today. She felt his eyes turn toward her. Runa maintained an expression of profound innocence for almost three seconds. Then she looked at him. One white eyebrow had risen. She nearly laughed. Kaelen's mouth slowly curved upward. But it was his eyes that betrayed him. Those piercing amber eyes settled on hers with an expression that made the warmth in her cheeks spread all the way to the tips of her ears. He understood perfectly. More than that, his entire expression seemed to say: Message received. And perhaps— I like your hips next to mine. Runa's carefully constructed innocence collapsed into a bashful grin. She looked away before he could see exactly how pleased she was. Unfortunately, she suspected he had already seen everything. They continued walking. Closer now. Neither mentioned it. That somehow made it better. But now their arms nearly brushed from time to time. The trail began climbing more sharply as the forest gradually thinned. The pines grew farther apart. Patches of blue sky opened between their branches, and the wind carried the cooler scent of stone from higher elevations. Then they rounded a bend and reached an opening in the trees. Runa slowed. An adjacent ridge rose beyond a shallow mountain ravine. Something stood upon its summit. At first she thought the jagged shapes were natural stone, but as she continued to stare she noticed more straight edges where nature seldom made them. Broken walls. Pillars. Arches. Ruins. Kaelen glanced back at her. There was a little satisfaction in his expression. “We're here.” Runa looked from him to the distant hill. “You brought me to ruins?” “You say that as though you're disappointed.” “Sorry” Runa said with an embarrassed tone “I'll try not to influence your judgment.” A narrow path descended from the opening of the trees where they were at, crossed the shallow ravine, and climbed toward the neighboring ridge. The final ascent was considerably steeper than Runa had expected. Half-buried stones appeared among the grass as they climbed. Then another. And another. Eventually Runa realized they were walking upon the remains of an ancient walk way. Cracked flagstones disappeared beneath centuries of soill. Portions of broad steps could still be seen beneath tangled roots and moss, leading toward the summit. The ruins slowly rose before them. And the closer Runa came, the quieter she became. The place was not simply old. It felt ancient. Two weathered pillars stood near the entrance, each thicker around than Runa could have wrapped her arms. Their capitals had broken away long ago, yet delicate carvings remained along their shafts. Spirals. Stars. Open hands lifted toward rays of light. Figures kneeling beneath what appeared to be an enormous circle surrounded by seven smaller points. Farther ahead, the remains of an arched entrance leaned precariously over the old road. Beyond it stood broken walls, fallen columns, courtyards overgrown with grass, and the fractured outlines of buildings whose roofs had disappeared centuries ago. A great rectangular stone lay half-swallowed by moss near the path. Across its face was carved a spiral surrounded by radiating lines. Runa stopped beside it. Vines threaded themselves through the grooves. She brushed several leaves aside to see the engravings more clearly. Then Kaelen said “We're close.” She stepped around the stone. “How many times have you been here?” “A few times.” Runa stared at him. “You neglected to mention that.” “You didn't ask.” She narrowed her eyes. There it was again. That quiet humor of his. “You're becoming troublesome.” “So I've been told.” “By whom?” Kaelen stepped over a fallen block. “You, mostly.” Runa laughed. They passed between the broken pillars. And the ruins opened around them. For a moment, even Runa had nothing clever to say. The ruins themselves were beautiful, but how beautiful it would have been to be here when it was first constructed. Not the beauty of a fortress, nor the excess of a palace intended to demonstrate wealth. This was different. Sacred. The remaining architecture possessed an elegance that seemed almost restrained. Pillars had been positioned in sweeping semicircles around open courtyards. Narrow channels carved into the stone suggested that water had once flowed between reflecting pools. Delicate geometric patterns bordered the surviving walls, intertwined with images of trees, stars, flames, and uplifted hands. At the center of one shattered wall, a weather-worn relief showed several figures standing beneath a great descending light. There were no crowns. No armies. No depictions of conquest. Only people looking upward. Runa slowed. The humor disappeared from her expression. There was something peaceful about the place. Something reverent. “This must have been beautiful when it was first built,” she said softly. Kaelen looked across the sanctuary. “I agree.” Runa stepped nearer the broken relief. “What was this place?” “A sanctuary.” “To whom?” Kaelen hesitated only briefly. “The Great One.” Runa looked toward him. “How can you tell?” He came closer and motioned toward the weathered carvings. “Partly because of the age. Most sanctuaries built in this region before the Collapse were dedicated to The Great One.” His finger moved toward the central figure of light. “But there are other signs.” Runa followed his gesture. “The open hands?” “Sometimes.” He pointed toward the circle surrounded by seven smaller stars. “That symbol appears in several surviving texts. The larger light represents The Great One, the one who set all things in motion. The smaller lights are usually understood to represent His works—or, depending on which historian you believe, those who carry His light into the world.” Runa examined it more carefully. “And the kneeling figures?” “I assume some form of worship.” Runa returned her attention to the sanctuary. The mountains had been reclaiming it for centuries. Grass grew between cracked flagstones. Wildflowers sprouted from fractures in the old courtyards. Young trees had rooted inside chambers whose ceilings had fallen away generations ago. One stone arch remained perfectly upright despite having no wall on either side, framing nothing but blue sky. Wind moved softly through the broken passages. For a few seconds, neither of them spoke. Runa wasn't certain why, but she felt reluctant to raise her voice here. The place did not feel abandoned. Not entirely. Perhaps stone remembered. Or perhaps sacred places held something of the prayers once spoken inside them. She rested her fingers lightly against one of the pillars. The carvings beneath them had been worn nearly smooth by time. “Who built it?” “No one knows.” Runa turned toward him with a kind indictment smile. “You're terrible at being a guide.” “I warned you I'd only been here a few times.” “No. You very specifically did not.” Kaelen smiled and continued through the ruins. Runa followed. “I've tried to research a bit about it in our local libraries, but no one seems to know too much about it.” “I didn't take you for a history buff” she said with a playful smirking grin. He smiled back amused, enjoying their playful banter. “I like learning things.” “That's a dangerously broad statement.” “Why?” “Because now I know how to bribe you.” “With books?” “Apparently.” “I prefer to use lor stones.” “But now I know you have a weakness.” Kaelen stepped around a section of fallen masonry. “I have several.” Runa raised an eyebrow. “Do you?” He looked at her. For just a moment, his gaze lingered. “Yes.” Then he continued walking. Runa stood still. Her lips parted slightly. Oh. Kaelen made it perhaps six steps before noticing she had stopped. He looked over his shoulder. “Coming?” Runa blinked. “Yes.” She hurried after him. Interesting. Very interesting. They moved deeper into the sanctuary. Kaelen seemed to know the safest route through the rubble, though he never hurried far ahead of her. Eventually they reached what had once been a lower courtyard. Most of the floor had collapsed centuries ago, leaving a depression perhaps eight or nine feet below the surrounding ruins. A massive pillar had fallen across it. Its base rested upon one side of the broken courtyard and its upper half against the opposite wall, forming a natural bridge. Kaelen stepped onto it without hesitation. He crossed easily. Runa followed to the edge. The pillar was broad enough. There was no real danger. Kaelen reached the other side and turned. His gaze flicked briefly toward the drop and then back to her. Not worried. Simply watchful. Runa smiled and sent. ~I've got this.~ Kaelen's expression remained perfectly innocent. ~I didn't say anything.~ ~You were thinking loudly.~ One corner of his mouth lifted. Runa stepped onto the fallen pillar. ~I'm not helpless, you know.~ ~I know.~ She crossed several steps. Kaelen remained where he was. When Runa was close enough he added, his voice calm and loving. “But while you're out here with me, I need to make sure you're safe.” Runa's next step became very deliberate. That irritating warmth returned to her cheeks. She reached the opposite side and stepped onto solid stone beside him. “I'll allow that.” “How generous.” “Don't abuse the privilege.” “I wouldn't dream of it.” The look in his eyes suggested he was enjoying this entirely too much. Runa gave him a playful glare and walked ahead. Kaelen followed. They passed around the remains of another wall. Then Runa saw what lay beyond it. She stopped. The world fell away. The sanctuary stood upon the edge of an immense mountain valley. Forests swept downward beneath them in great rolling layers of green. A silver river wound through the distant lowlands, disappearing between hills and reappearing miles beyond like a ribbon cast carelessly across the earth. Farther still, mountains rose behind mountains. The nearest were dark with pine. The next softened into deep blue. Beyond those stood pale ranges veiled in distance until the final peaks were little more than brushstrokes beneath the sky. Cloud shadows drifted slowly across the valley floor. The sun had begun its gradual descent toward the western mountains, warming the entire landscape with amber light. Runa forgot whatever she had been about to say. The wind caught her dark hair and lifted it away from her face. “Wow,” she whispered. The word escaped with a long breath. Behind her, Kaelen said nothing. Runa walked farther into the open courtyard. A low section of ruined wall marked the edge before the ground sloped sharply downward. She approached it, but stopped well short of anything that would turn admiration of scenery into foolishness. For several seconds she simply stood there. There were places in the world that seemed determined to remind a person how small they were. This was one of them. But strangely, Runa did not feel insignificant. She felt grateful. She thought of the beauty bestowed upon the earth by The Great One. Of the hands that had shaped these stones centuries before either she or Kaelen had been born. Of the people who had once stood here and looked upon this same valley. Perhaps they too had delighted in this high mountain wind. Perhaps they had prayed here. Perhaps they had wondered what would become of the world after they were gone. A quiet warmth settled over her. Not an answer. Not even a thought she could easily put into words. Just peace. Runa smiled. Then she remembered the man who had brought her here. She turned. “Why didn't you tell me about this place?” Kaelen stood several paces behind her. And he wasn't looking at the valley. He was looking at her. Runa stopped. For once, Kaelen seemed to have forgotten himself. The expression on his face was almost the same as the one she imagined she had worn when first seeing that magnificent view. Wonder. Warmth. Something quieter and more intimate beneath both. His amber eyes held hers. Runa suddenly became very aware of the wind moving through her hair. And of the fact that she had caught him staring, and seemingly lost in it. A smile tugged at her lips. “What?” Kaelen blinked, and seemed to regain his composure. The expression disappeared beneath his usual composure so quickly that Runa might have doubted seeing it at all. Almost. He smiled at her, with a slight bit of embarrassment. An expression not common for Kelen. Runa treasured it immediately. Then he looked toward the mountains. Runa's smile widened. Kaelen started walking towards them. “Come on,” he gestured Runa remained where she was. “Kaelen.” she said in an attempt to keep what was happening from ending. He stopped and looked back at her. Kaelen motioned farther along the ruins. “Over here. I have a better spot.” Runa studied him for another moment, and with a tingling warmth in her that was not coming from the sun,she followed him. Her smile remaining firmly fixed in place. Perhaps the ruins weren't going to be the most interesting discovery on the mountain today.
